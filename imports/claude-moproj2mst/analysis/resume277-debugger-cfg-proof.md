## Proven local order, and its limit

In `wave3-earlier-save-140051190.asm`, the task path helper call at `0x511b9` returns to `0x511be`; the caller then gets the Project pointer at `0x511c3` and directly calls creator `0x8c630` at `0x511cb`, returning to `0x511d0`. In `wave3-earlier-save-14008c630.asm`, creator begins at `0x8c630`, allocates `0xb8` bytes, and makes five direct calls to helper `0x81afb0` at `0x8c66d`, `0x8c6a6`, `0x8c6eb`, `0x8c730`, and `0x8c775`. Their normal next-instruction PCs are `0x8c672`, `0x8c6ab`, `0x8c6f0`, `0x8c735`, and `0x8c77a`. The fifth call loads DL=1 at `0x8c76b` and its QString pointer into RCX at `0x8c76d`. The optional branch starts only after the fifth call and its QString destructor: Project subobject lookup at `0x8c78e`, null test at `0x8c798`, predicate at `0x8c7a5`, optional helper call at `0x8c7c8`, and normal return at `0x8c7e0`. The helper returns are not tested by this creator. The selected fifth call is therefore ordered after the four earlier *normal* helper returns on this local path; the branch at `0x8c79e` cannot bypass it after creator entry along normal intra-function flow.

This is a local, conditional dominance statement. It assumes execution stays on the displayed normal control flow and all intervening calls return to their listed continuations. It does not establish that creator is entered in the positive-ID B98 child, that the fifth QString is the clone's `Lubricants` path, or that the helper returns normally. `wave3-earlier-save-14081afb0.asm` shows a branch at `0x81b046/0x81b056` and, on the warning path, an indirect imported call at `0x81b24e` before helper's `ret` at `0x81b2a8`; the retained helper/dialog qualification identifies this as possible modal work. Creator also has imported destructor calls after each helper. These are transfers outside the short creator CFG. The copied windows contain no complete call-target closure or proof about callback, other-thread, exception/unwind, or reentrant entry edges.

## Blind intervals that remain unproved

CP269's optimistic machine keeps outer entry `0x1ca30`, creator entry `0x8c630`, helper entry `0x81afb0`, and one frontier slot during the outer phase. Its frontier moves through selected duplicate `0xc78c10` (caller return `0x1cf11`), factory `0xc79390` (`0x1d08a`), cast `0x146f8fc` (`0x1d0bd`), Project accessor `0x44a90` (`0x1d0d8`), and acceptance `0x8a9b0` (`0x1d0fb`) entries and their matching return PCs. While one is armed as the frontier return, the other entries are disarmed. Each omitted selected-caller entry at the outer frame's qualified RSP could violate CP214 order or pending state. The outer decompile in `wave2-doe-output-default-task-open-and-manager-dtor-decompile.json` confirms a direct sequential path, but also calls outside that window, including a virtual call after acceptance, and dialog branches. It does not prove absence of all nested or cross-thread selected-caller entries across the dynamic interval.

While acceptance is active, creator entry `0x8c630` must be selected by return `0x511d0`, RCX=the accepted P and the same child/thread/frame. While creator is active, helper entry `0x81afb0` is selected by return `0x8c77a`, DL=1 and an exact clone path. CP269 reuses the frontier for creator and helper normal returns. The local creator CFG shows the fifth direct call, but helper `0x81afb0` is a shared function called at least five times by this creator and once from the task caller at `0x511b9`; an entry breakpoint must remain available to distinguish caller returns and nested/repeated entries while a prior helper span is pending. A return-site breakpoint alone cannot detect an extra helper entry that completes before the selected return. The retained CP214 agent's accepted-frame, pending/order and overlap semantics (`outer-loader-observers.js`, lines 103-155 and 171-217) cannot be replaced by a simple positive-path chronology. There is no complete proof that helper, creator or outer entry cannot reenter through their callees, imported modal path, exception machinery, or another owned thread.

At selected activation entry in the postOpen phase, the five distinct simultaneous obligations identified by CP269 remain `0x1ca30` outer entry, `0x2a0e0` postOpen entry, `0x45780` current entry, `0xa7250` activation entry, and `0x2aaa1` activation normal return. Their relevant filters are respectively the main caller `0x1c94` plus invocation state, postOpen caller `0x1cae` plus same main/P, current caller `0x2aa72` plus postOpen RSP minus `0x4e0`, activation caller `0x2aaa1` plus same frame/P and observed EDX=0, and return RSP equal to activation entry RSP+8 on the same thread. A nested postOpen entry and a repeated activation entry poison before caller filtering in the retained agent. A selected current entry while activation is pending poisons order. A later qualifying outer entry changes invocation serial. CP214's static postOpen review establishes the ordinary synchronous call sites `0x2aa6d` and `0x2aa9c` and the ordinary return order, not exclusivity while arbitrary callees or virtual callbacks execute. Four per-thread DR execution comparators cannot cover those five distinct addresses. Even a proof about the `0x8c630` creator body alone would leave this independent postOpen conflict unresolved.

The retained direct calls establish neither whole-program entry exclusivity nor a closed-world call graph. In particular, the `0x81b24e` modal call and the outer/postOpen virtual dispatches are unresolved for this proof; exception/unwind exits do not satisfy a normal-return condition; and another owned thread cannot borrow a comparator from the primary thread. The current artifacts also do not establish that all selected-caller return addresses are unique to one dynamic frame without entry RSP/TID checks. Static reachability could at most justify removing a guard after all such edges and the interval's ownership/thread assumptions are proved. It could never establish a same-child runtime creator/helper occurrence, actual `mkpath` causation, or instrumentation neutrality.
